# Blogs & Articles

Blogs & Articles writes long-form pieces for your own website: SEO-ready blog posts and in-depth articles in your identity's voice, researched on the web and in your knowledge base, with their sources cited. You edit them in a document editor, then copy or export them into your website.

## Start a blog or article

Click **New blog or article**, or **Write a blog** on an idea, and fill in what you want:

| Field                  | What it does                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Type**               | **Blog post**: conversational and practical, written to the reader, with an FAQ. **Article**: authoritative and in-depth, with more sources, context and counter-arguments. Your voice settings still decide whether it says "I" or "we" and how formal it is. |
| **Topic**              | What it is about. From an idea, it starts as the idea's title and angle, and the idea's sources are used too. |
| **Primary keyword**    | The phrase readers search for. Leave it empty and the AI picks one.                                           |
| **Secondary keywords** | Related phrases to use as well, up to 10.                                                                     |
| **Length**             | Short (about 800 words), Medium (about 1,500) or Long (about 2,500).                                          |
| **Writing style**      | How-to guide, List post, Explainer (educational), Comparison, Industry analysis, Thought leadership, Opinion, News analysis or Case study. Each is built its own way: a comparison leads with a table and a verdict, a how-to with its steps. |
| **The real case**      | For a case study only, and required: who the customer is, the problem, what was done and the results. Nothing else about the case is written. |
| **Who it is for**      | All your audiences, one of them, or someone else you describe.                                                |
| **Goal**               | Its call to action closes the piece, and may link to the goal's page once.                                    |
| **Sources**            | Research the web, use your knowledge base, and include an FAQ. Blog posts and articles both start with an FAQ, except opinion pieces, news analysis and case studies. |
| **Extra instructions** | Anything else, such as an offer to mention or words to avoid.                                                 |

Then click **Generate**. Writing usually takes 2-3 minutes. You can leave the page while it works; the blog shows **Writing…** in the list until it is ready.

## How it is written

1. **Research**: the web is searched for facts, figures, definitions and examples on the topic. Every source is read on its own page: a search result whose page cannot be read is left out and noted, so nothing in the piece rests on a search snippet. A figure reported second-hand is traced to the original publisher where it can be. Only words copied exactly from the pages are kept, with the sentences around them for context, and each source is rated for how far it can be trusted. Pages written to rank in search, and pages about another market than your readers', are left out. An article's research goes further, looking for background, evidence that cuts the other way, and statements from recognised authorities.
2. **What people search**: at the same time, the questions people ask Google about the topic (**People also ask**) and its related searches are collected. Without a primary keyword, the phrases readers would type are worked out first. These questions shape the section headings and the FAQ; their answers come only from the sources.
3. **Knowledge base**: the passages of your knowledge documents closest to the topic, without the menus and footers of web pages. Demo screens on your website are treated as sample data, never as real orders or customers.
4. **Plan**: before any writing, the piece is planned: what the person searching wants (the search intent), its angle and what it adds that other articles on the topic do not, a structure that suits the style, which searched question each section answers, which source backs each point and which example makes it concrete, the people, organisations and terms it must name and define, where a table, diagram or picture helps, whether your products help with this topic's problem at all (they are left out when they don't), the call to action and the pages of your site a reader of each section would want next.
5. **Writing**: from the plan, in your voice, for your audience and goal, following your guardrails and banned words. Every section opens with a short, direct answer that makes sense on its own, so search engines and AI assistants can quote it; the details follow. Figures say who measured them, among whom and when, once, where they first appear. What the sources establish is stated plainly, and only views people genuinely dispute are marked as yours. A term the piece is about is defined early. FAQ answers open with the answer itself. A made-up scenario only appears in a box labelled **Hypothetical example**. The last section ends on what to decide or do next, not on a summary or a list of caveats.
6. **Pictures**: made while the text is written, in your brand's colours and one shared look, with no text in them: the featured image, and up to three pictures inside sections where seeing something helps (none in a section that already has a table or diagram). Each has alt text and a caption. A picture that cannot be made in time is left out and noted; the piece is complete without it.
7. **Checks**: every sentence that links a source is checked against what that source says, and every figure must come from the source it links or from your own details. Sentences that fail are rewritten, and so are sentences that credit an organisation or quote words no source has, rest a figure on a weak source, repeat another site's words, talk about the research instead of the subject, restate a figure already given, use generic AI wording, or end the piece on a question. A link whose source does not support its sentence is taken off and noted. The SEO checklist and the quality checks are run too.

Facts are named and linked to the page they came from. The **References** at the end list only the sources that support what the text says. A blog links only to its sources, your goal's page, and your own website and offerings: any other link is removed, and noted under **SEO**.

## Edit it

The document is on the left and its details are on the right.

- **Title**: the page's main heading.
- **Featured image**: above the title, with its alt text (what the picture shows, for screen readers and search engines) and an optional caption. **Make again** makes a new one, with what to change if you like; **Remove** takes it off; **Download** saves it.
- **Text**: format it with the toolbar: paragraph, section heading (H2), subheading (H3), bold, italic, lists, quotes, links, **Table** (insert one, add or delete rows and columns) and **Box** (a Hypothetical example, Key takeaways, Tip, Watch out or Note). Pasted tables stay tables; pasted images are not kept.
- **Diagrams and charts**: drawn from their words and numbers, so they are always spelled right. Point at one and click **Edit** to change its steps, values, caption or alt text, or remove it.
- **Section pictures**: point at one and click **Edit** to change its alt text and caption, **Make again** for a new picture of the same subject (with what to change, if you like), or the bin to take it out of the text. The picture it replaces stays under **Versions**.
- **FAQ**: the questions and answers under the text. Add, reorder or remove them.
- **Save**, or <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd> + <Kbd>S</Kbd> (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd> + <Kbd>S</Kbd> on a Mac), saves your changes. If the blog was changed in another tab meanwhile, you are asked to reload instead, so nothing is overwritten.

### The tabs beside the text

- **SEO**: the meta title, meta description, URL slug, keywords and search intent, how the page shows in search results, and the SEO checklist, which updates as you type. Below it, the quality checks update when you save, in four groups:
  - **Accuracy**: figures in no source, figures their linked source does not give, organisations credited or words quoted that no source has.
  - **Sources**: figures resting only on a forum or a social post, figures more than five years old given without their year, too few independent publishers, or only your own pages cited.
  - **Writing**: repeated phrases, figures and sentence openings, an FAQ that repeats the text, sentences about the research instead of the subject, "in our view" used more than twice, generic AI wording, a stock opening line, a piece that ends on a question, and whether it reads as a blog post or an article should.
  - **Search & AI answers**: sections that do not open with a direct answer, FAQ answers that do not open with one, no heading asking a question people search, planned names missing, no early definition, and keyword stuffing.
- **Outline**: the headings in order. Click one to jump to it, or **Rewrite** to have one section written again, with what to change.
- **Sources**: what it was written from, which sources it cites, how far each can be trusted, whether its words were read on the page or only in a search result, and how each cited sentence held up against its source. **Check before publishing** lists anything left to look at. After editing, **Check sources again** checks the saved text.
- **Links**: pages of your website and your published blogs the piece links to, or could. Select words in the text and click **Insert** to link them, or insert the suggested words at the cursor.
- **Versions**: earlier versions of the text, each kept when a save, regeneration, section rewrite or restore replaced it. **Preview** one, or **Restore** it; the text it replaces is kept too.
- **Brief**: the plan it was written from (with the names it defines and the pictures it planned), the questions people search and where each is answered, what you asked for and what it has cost. **Start a new blog from this brief** opens the form with the same answers.

### Write it again

**Regenerate** plans and writes the whole piece again from its brief. Say what to change, or leave it empty for a different take. It reuses the sources and searched questions it found last time unless you tick **Research the web again**, and keeps its pictures unless you tick **Make new pictures**: the featured image stays, and each section picture moves to the new section that matches it, with your alt text and caption. A rewritten section keeps its picture too. The current text is kept as an earlier version.

## Preview, copy and export

- **Preview** shows the page as readers will see it, with its search result. **Edit** goes back to the editor.
- **Copy formatted** copies the article with its headings, lists, links, tables and pictures, ready to paste into WordPress, Webflow, Google Docs or Word. **Copy Markdown** and **Copy HTML** copy it in those formats, with pictures named by their files. **Copy structured data** copies the data search engines read, for your page's custom code.
- **Export** downloads it as Markdown (with its title, meta description, slug, search intent and keywords at the top), as a complete HTML page (pictures included, with its meta and social tags and its structured data), as a **PDF** (your browser's print window opens: choose **Save as PDF** as the destination), as plain text, or its **Pictures**: the featured image, each section picture and each diagram as an image file. Files are named after the URL slug. The HTML page and the PDF show who wrote it and when it was last updated under the title.

The structured data describes the piece as a blog post or an article, with its author, dates, the works it cites, and what it is about and mentions. FAQ data is included only when the FAQ is on the page, and the page's address only once you mark it published with its live URL. Pictures are not listed in it, since they are stored privately: when you upload them to your CMS with the post, the CMS adds its own picture addresses.

What you copy or export is what is on screen, unsaved changes included.

## Share a link

**Share** makes a public link to the blog, for a client, a colleague or a reviewer who has no account. Anyone with the link can read it in their browser without signing in, with its pictures, diagrams and byline, as the exported HTML page shows it. Search engines are asked not to list it.

- The link shows the last saved version and follows your later saves: unsaved changes appear once you save them.
- **Copy link** copies it and **Open** opens it in a new tab. The button reads **Shared** while the link is on.
- **Stop sharing** turns the link off for everyone at once. Sharing again makes a new link; the old one stays off. Deleting the blog turns it off too.

## Mark it published

Blogs go out by hand: you paste it into your website or CMS, then record where it went.

1. Click **More** and choose **Mark as published…**.
2. Under **Where is it published?**, pick **Your website**, **WordPress**, **Webflow**, **Shopify** or **Other**.
3. Paste its **Live URL** if you like.
4. Check **Published on**: it starts at today. Pick an earlier day if it went out before. A day in the future is not accepted.
5. Click **Mark as published**.

The blog then shows where it went, such as **Published on WordPress**, and it appears in [Published Content](/docs/publishing/published-content) with your other published content.

To change these details later, click **More**, choose **Edit publishing details…**, and click **Save details**. **Mark as a draft** undoes publishing and clears where and when it went out.

WordPress, Webflow and Shopify are where a blog is published, not kinds of content. The app does not publish to them for you.

## What it costs

A blog with web research usually costs $0.35-0.70, more for a long article; each picture is about $0.06-0.17 of that. Rewriting one section or checking the sources again costs a few cents, and a new featured image or section picture $0.06-0.17. It counts towards your AI spending limits under **Blogs & articles**.

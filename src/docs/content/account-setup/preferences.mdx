# Preferences

Preferences decide how many ideas a run proposes, which platforms and formats posts are written for, how each platform sounds, and what posts must stay away from.

Emoji and hashtags for LinkedIn are set in [Voice](/docs/account-setup/voice), and where a link goes is set on each goal. The automatic run schedule has its own [Schedule](/docs/publishing/schedule) page.

## See your preferences in context

### Set how many ideas a run proposes

**Ideas per run** is 5 unless you change it. The other preferences follow below it.

## Ideas

### Ideas per run

Choose **3**, **4**, **5**, **6**, **8**, **10** or **12 ideas**. The default is 5.

A run can return more ideas than this when you pick several goals for it: each goal picked gets to lead at least one idea, up to 12 ideas in a run.

### Call to action

On by default. When it is on, every draft closes with a call to action: something the reader can do or answer. When it is off, a call to action is optional and is only added when one follows naturally from the post.

## Platforms

### Formats

Turn on the formats posts are written in. Each idea is written separately for every platform you pick when you create posts.

| Platform  | Formats                  | Length                      |
| --------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| LinkedIn  | **LinkedIn post**        | Up to 3,000 characters      |
| X         | **X post**, **X thread** | Up to 280 characters a post |
| Threads   | **Threads post**         | Up to 500 characters        |
| Facebook  | **Facebook post**        | Up to 2,000 characters      |
| Instagram | **Instagram post**       | Up to 2,200 characters      |

Keep at least one format on; with none, you see "Turn on at least one format."

The platforms you turn on are the ones the identity posts on. They also decide which profile link fields your [Profile](/docs/account-setup/profile) shows.

## Style per platform

Each platform with a format turned on gets a style:

| Style            | Posts are                                       |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Professional** | Expertise, insight and opinions                 |
| **Casual**       | Relaxed and personal, like talking to followers |
| **Fun**          | Jokes, skits, POV and trends                    |

LinkedIn starts as **Professional** and every other platform as **Casual**. Professional posts share expertise; casual and fun ones are written for the people who follow you. The mix of styles across your platforms also steers the kind of ideas a run proposes.

### Details for each platform

LinkedIn takes its audience and tone from your Profile and Voice. Every other platform you post on has its own box:

- **Who follows you here** (optional): who you reach there, in your own words. For example "Customers and local followers, not the partners on LinkedIn".
- **How you sound here** (optional): how you sound there beyond your core voice. For example "Lighter, more personal, first names, the odd emoji".
- **Emoji**: **None**, **As bullets** or **Freely**. Left on **Default**, casual and fun platforms use emoji freely, and professional ones follow your Voice setting, as on LinkedIn.
- **How you write on** the platform: a style learned from your own posts, described below.

### Learn how you write on a platform

Optional. Paste a few of your real posts, and drafts for that platform will follow how you write there, not just your core voice.

1. In the platform's box, click **Learn from your** … **posts** (for example **Learn from your Instagram posts**).
2. Paste 2 or more of your real posts, with a line of --- between them. The count of posts found updates as you paste.
3. Click **Learn my style**.

Up to 12 posts are used. The learned style then shows with how many posts it came from, and drafts for that platform follow it where it differs from your core voice. Learning uses AI, which counts toward your [AI usage](/docs/help/ai-usage).

<Tags type="note">

Learning a style is saved straight away, apart from the page's **Save** button, and **Cancel** does not undo it. **Learn again** replaces the posts it learned from before. **Forget** removes the style and those posts, and that platform's posts go back to your core voice.

</Tags>

## Things to avoid

### Topics to avoid

Subjects posts must never be about, separated by commas, for example "politics, competitor pricing". Repeats are saved once. Ideas and drafts are told to stay clear of them, and a draft that names one is flagged in its checks.

### Rules posts must follow

Optional. Any rules every post must follow, for example "Never mention client names without approval." Ideas and drafts are given these notes as compliance rules.

## Save your preferences

Click **Save** in the header. You see "Content preferences saved successfully." Once saved, the page opens read-only: click **Edit** to change it, and **Cancel** to put back what was saved.

If you used the AI setup before saving preferences for the first time, its suggestions for **Topics to avoid** and **Rules posts must follow** may already be filled in. A banner says what was filled, with **Undo**. Nothing is saved until you click **Save**.

<Details summary="Why did a run give me more ideas than I asked for?">

**Ideas per run** is raised when a run serves more goals than that number, so that every goal leads at least one idea. A run never keeps more than 12.

</Details>

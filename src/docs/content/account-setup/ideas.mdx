# Ideas

The Ideas page, titled **Idea Review & Approval**, is where you decide which ideas from your runs become content: social media posts, or a blog or article. Approving an idea writes nothing; nothing is written until you ask for it.

The number next to **Ideas** in the sidebar is how many ideas of the active identity are waiting for review. It goes down as you approve or reject them.

## Find the ideas you want

The page opens on **All statuses**. Use the status menu to show one status; each option shows its count.

| Status        | Meaning                                                             |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **To review** | Proposed by a run and waiting for your decision.                    |
| **Approved**  | Approved, but no post has been written yet.                         |
| **Drafting…** | A post is being written from it. Only shown under **All statuses**. |
| **Drafted**   | Has posts. **View in Posts** opens them; **More posts** adds more.  |
| **Rejected**  | Set aside. You can still approve it later.                          |

If you post on more than one platform, tabs let you show **All platforms** or one platform's ideas. Ideas from different runs are grouped under a heading with the run's date and goals. The filters are kept in the page address, so reloading or sharing the link keeps them.

## Read an idea card

The front of a card shows the goal it serves (the main goal first, then any other after a +), its title, what the post leaves the reader with and the platforms it suits. Two badges can appear:

- **On trend**: it rides something happening now (trend relevance of 75 or more).
- **Weak sources**: its facts rest on sources that cannot be trusted much. Hover to read why.

Click **Details** for the angle, main point, hook, why now, argument and why it is not generic. **Check before approving** lists problems found while checking its facts, and **Evidence** lists each fact with its source and how far that kind of source can be trusted.

## The scorecard

**How it scores** rates the idea from 0 to 100 on each of these, with a one-line reason:

| Score                    | What it measures                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Goal fit**             | Whether it would achieve its goal: the right readers, the goal's message landed, a reason to take the goal's action.                                                 |
| **Engagement potential** | How likely its readers on that platform are to stop, react, comment, share or save it.                                                                               |
| **Originality**          | How far it is from what your field and you already post. If it is close in meaning to one of your earlier ideas, the score is capped and the reason names that idea. |
| **Trend relevance**      | Whether it rides something happening now. Not a verdict: an evergreen idea is not a weak one.                                                                        |
| **Brand fit**            | Whether it is recognisably yours: your world, your voice, your guardrails and the platform's norms.                                                                  |
| **Source quality**       | How far its facts can be trusted. Shows **Not needed** when it states no facts that need a source.                                                                   |

Scores of 75 or more show green, 55 to 74 amber and below 55 red; trend relevance always shows in a neutral colour. Scores estimate potential and cannot promise reach. Ideas scoring below 50 on goal fit, or written for the wrong readers, are dropped before they reach this page.

### How source quality works

Source quality is worked out from the sources, not judged by the AI. Each kind of source has a trust level, from **Your own details**, **Your knowledge base** and **Official source** at the top, through **Established news**, **Industry source**, **Reference work** and **Newsletter**, down to **Social post**, **Community discussion** and **Low-quality source**. **Unrated site** is a site the app does not know: check it before relying on it.

The score is the average over all the idea's facts. A fact its source only partly states counts for less, and a fact no source backs counts as zero and is removed from the idea. Below 55, the card shows **Weak sources**.

## Approve, reject or edit an idea

- **Approve** takes one click and moves the idea to Approved. It does not write a post.
- **Reject** asks "Reject this idea?"; click **Reject** to confirm. You can approve it again later. Future runs treat rejected ideas as earlier work and will not propose them again.
- **Edit hook & angle**, under Details, changes the hook and the one-line angle. Click **Save changes**.

Once an idea is being drafted or has been drafted, these buttons go away. To approve every idea waiting for review, click **Approve all** (it shows the count) and confirm. On a platform tab, only that platform's ideas are approved.

## Turn approved ideas into posts

Posts are written only from selected ideas that are approved, or already drafted.

1. Tick the ideas you want. **Select all** (or **Select page** when there is more than one page) ticks every idea on screen that can be ticked (drafted or rejected ones only on their own status tabs), switches to show the selected count beside the checkbox, and can be clicked again to clear the selection; **Select 3 approved** (with your count) ticks every approved idea. Ticks stay when you change page or status, but not platform tab.
2. Click **Create 3 posts** (the count follows your selection). If some ticked ideas are still to review, the button reads **Approve & Create Posts** and approves them first.
3. In the dialog, pick the platforms and the type of post, and whether to add an image. Each idea gets a post on every platform you pick that it has no post on yet. Click **Create posts**.

A single approved idea also has its own **Generate Post** button.

When the posts are ready you see "Posts generated" and the Social Media Posts page opens. An idea that could not be drafted goes back to Approved, or stays Drafted if it already had posts, and **Try again** retries it. To get more ideas, click **Generate ideas** to start a new run.

### Add platforms to a drafted idea

A drafted idea can still get posts on other platforms. Its card lists the platforms it has posts on under **Drafted**. Tick it, or click **More posts** on its card, then pick platforms in the dialog as above.

In the dialog, a platform every selected idea already has a post on shows **Already drafted** and cannot be picked. One that only some of them have shows how many, such as **Drafted for 1 of 3**, and the rest get a post there. An idea never gets a second post on the same platform, and its existing posts are left as they are. A Reel or a Short counts on its own: an idea with an Instagram post can still get a Reel.

## Write a blog from an idea

Click **Write a blog** on any idea that is not rejected. The new blog form opens with the idea's title, angle, goal and sources filled in. Writing a blog does not change the idea's status. See [Blogs & Articles](/docs/publishing/blogs).

## See the runs behind your ideas

Ideas come from runs. To see every run, with its research, the ideas it produced and what it cost, click **Run Details** at the top of the page. See [Run Details](/docs/publishing/run-details).
